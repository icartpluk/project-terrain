# Mission and feasibility

## Primary use case
Investigate whether remotely operated humanoid robotic platforms could reduce human exposure during **authorized** response and remediation at damaged underground nuclear facilities, using the Iran enriched-uranium recovery scenario as a motivating example.

## Engineering questions
1. Can a humanoid platform traverse representative unstable and obstructed test environments?
2. Can operators maintain situational awareness when GPS and normal communications are unavailable?
3. Can the system recognize hazards and halt safely when information is insufficient?
4. Can specialized, independently certified tools and containment systems be integrated without compromising platform stability?
5. Can human operators and independent safety personnel maintain positive control?

## Critical feasibility gaps
- No demonstrated evidence in this project that Tesla Optimus is radiation-hardened or qualified for nuclear-material handling.
- Underground communications, localization, mobility, load capacity, battery life, decontamination, and fail-safe control require experimental validation.
- Radiation measurement, nuclear-material identification, containment, and recovery require qualified specialist systems and regulatory oversight.
- Humanoid form may not outperform wheeled, tracked, or purpose-built remote systems; comparisons are necessary.

## Boundaries
This is a high-level research proposal, not a site-specific operational plan. No instructions for locating, extracting, packaging, or moving enriched uranium are included.

## Secondary application
Mars-oriented robotic excavation, mapping, and infrastructure preparation may share non-nuclear capabilities, subject to separate environmental qualification.
